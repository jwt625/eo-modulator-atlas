## Findings

### Numerical

**R2-F1 (numerical). lin2025a-mzi `vpi_dc_v` = 31.0 (basis derived, qualifier approx) is a batch-computed value the paper never reports, extrapolated beyond the measured voltage range.**
- Cells: data/devices.csv lin2025a-mzi `vpi_dc_v` 31.0, `vpi_basis` derived, `qualifiers` vpi_dc_v:approx, `vpi_convention` per_arm_phase_shifter; data/evidence/lin2025a.yaml `derived` item vpi_dc_v (formula FSR/(2 x tuning)).
- Source: p.3 text: FSR 2.563 nm, 0.288 nm shift at 5 V, 41.3 pm/V slope from 0 to 20 V (Fig. 2(d), p.4: points at about 0, 290, 415, 690, 830 pm for 0/5/10/15/20 V, my reading). No Vpi, no VpiL anywhere in the main text; the paper's figure of merit is r42 = 1268 pm/V. A pi shift needs FSR/2 = 1.28 nm, i.e. about 31 V, which is above the largest applied voltage (20 V, 0.83 nm shift), so the number is an extrapolation as well as batch arithmetic, and the paper's own 5 V point gives 22.2 V.
- Why: SKILL hard rule 1 (a number comes from the primary text or a figure/table of it, or it stays empty) and rule 4 (do not compute Vpi-type figures the paper does not report; views derive them). Convention (h) reserves basis `derived` for values the authors compute. The row will plot as a measured-device Vpi of 31 V in Vpi comparisons; the approx qualifier does not convey the 22-31 V spread or the extrapolation.
- Proposed change: clear `vpi_dc_v`, `vpi_basis` and the `vpi_dc_v:approx` qualifier on lin2025a-mzi; remove the `vpi_dc_v` item from the evidence `derived` list (or keep it there only if the validator tolerates a derived item without a CSV cell, otherwise move the arithmetic into the row note); keep `fsr_nm` 2.563 and `tuning_nm_per_v` 0.0413 (both stated) and keep the 22-31 V arithmetic and the "above the 20 V measured range" caveat in the row note. `drive` single_ended can stay; `vpi_convention` may stay or be cleared (no Vpi left to qualify).

**R2-F2 (numerical). mao2024-1550 `bw3db_ghz` 70 (gt), `bw_basis` measured: the ">70 GHz" statement refers to a low-frequency-compensated S21, which the row does not say.**
- Cells: data/devices.csv mao2024-1550 `bw3db_ghz` 70, `bw_measured_to_ghz` 70, `bw_basis` measured, `qualifiers` bw3db_ghz:gt, `notes` (no mention of compensation); evidence entry bw3db_ghz (basis measured; its note does mention the compensation).
- Source: p.4 text: "the EO response changed sharply between 1 and 8 GHz, as indicated by the blue line ... attributable to parasitic surface conduction in the SiO2/Si substrate ... by compensating for the electrical properties in the low-frequency region, a broad frequency response from 1 to 70 GHz could be achieved ... as indicated by the red line"; Fig. 3(b) caption "bandwidth larger than 70 GHz". Fig. 3(b) (embedded image, approximate reading): the raw blue trace sits about +3 to +6 dB at 1-5 GHz and about -2 dB at 70 GHz, i.e. more than 3 dB below its own low-frequency level by roughly 35 GHz; the compensated red trace starts near 0 dB, touches about -3 to -3.5 dB in noise near 55-56 GHz and ends near -2.5 dB at 70 GHz.
- Why: the bound is the authors' statement about post-processed data, not a raw measured response; per convention (h) such author-processed values are `derived`, and the row-level consumer sees "measured >70 GHz" without the caveat.
- Proposed change: keep `bw3db_ghz` 70 with `bw3db_ghz:gt` and `bw_measured_to_ghz` 70 (paper's stated bound, convention (c)); set `bw_basis` and the evidence basis of bw3db_ghz to `derived`; add to the row note: "Bandwidth bound is on the authors' low-frequency-compensated S21 (red, Fig. 3(b), p.4); raw response shows a 1-8 GHz excess from substrate surface conduction; compensated trace reaches about -3 dB near 55 GHz in noise (visual)."

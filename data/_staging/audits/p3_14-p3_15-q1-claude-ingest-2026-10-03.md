### Numerical

**F1 (numerical). sabatti2024-sh `bw_measured_to_ghz` = 60 is wrong for the SH trace.**
- Row/field: `data/_staging/p3_14/devices.csv` sabatti2024-sh `bw_measured_to_ghz` 60; evidence entry sabatti2024-sh `bw_measured_to_ghz` 60 ("VNA sweep 20 MHz to 60 GHz", p.3 Fig. 3(c)).
- Paper: p.3 Sec. 3B: sweep 20 MHz to 60 GHz, "measure a bandwidth for the FH beyond our setup capabilities and a bandwidth of 40 GHz for the SH ... limited by the noise floor of the VNA". Fig. 3(c) (300 dpi crop): the 768 nm (SH) trace starts at 0 GHz and ends at 40 GHz; only the 1536 nm trace continues to 60 GHz.
- Correction: set sabatti2024-sh `bw_measured_to_ghz` = 40 (convention (c): the usable measured-to value for that trace) and fix the evidence entry and note. The FH row (60) is right.

**F2 (numerical). sabatti2024-sh `bw3db_ghz:gt` conflicts with the plotted SH trace; state the dips.**
- Row/field: sabatti2024-sh `bw3db_ghz` 40 with qualifier `gt`.
- Paper: abstract p.1 "measured high bandwidth of 40 GHz"; p.3 "limited by the noise floor of the VNA and not by the working principle". Fig. 3(c): the SH trace reaches the dashed -3 dB line at about 30 GHz, goes below it at about 34-37 GHz (minimum about -3.9 dB near 36 GHz), then recovers to about -0.3 dB at 40 GHz. The batch notes mention the dips but the cell asserts a lower bound.
- Correction: either enter 40 as the authors' claimed value without `gt` (with `bw_measured_to_ghz` = 40, F1) and put the dips and the VNA-noise-floor attribution in the evidence note, or keep `gt` and quote the dips explicitly in the evidence entry; do not leave the bound unexplained. Author-claimed value vs noisy trace is the case the batch's SPEC_PROPOSALS already raise.

**F3 (numerical). shamsansari2021-a `bw_measured_to_ghz` = 50 and the "stays near 0 dB to 50 GHz" description overstate Fig. 4(d).**
- Row/field: shamsansari2021-a `bw3db_ghz` 50 (approx), `bw_measured_to_ghz` 50; row notes "trace ... stays near 0 dB to 50 GHz with fluctuations beyond 45 GHz".
- Paper: Fig. 4(d) caption p.3 "3-dB bandwidth of ~50 GHz"; p.3 text "The response fluctuation beyond 45 GHz is due to the limited bandwidth of the photodiode" (45 GHz New Focus 1014; VNA 50 GHz). Fig. 4(d) (450 dpi crop): the trace stays within about 0 to -2.5 dB up to about 40 GHz, then drifts to a mean of about -4 dB and dips to about -5.5 dB at 44-48 GHz, ending near -1.5 to -5 dB at 50 GHz; it is below -3 dB for most of 43-50 GHz.
- Correction: keep 50 only as the authors' captioned claim (`approx` is fine); set `bw_measured_to_ghz` to 45 (the photodiode limit the authors state) or add `gt` with 45, and rewrite the note to say the trace falls below -3 dB beyond about 43 GHz and the authors attribute this to the photodiode.

**F4 (numerical). guo2026-a `optical_power_handling_dbm` 11.5: wrong basis and an unsupported derivation in the evidence note.**
- Row/field: guo2026-a `optical_power_handling_dbm` 11.5 (qualifier approx); evidence entry basis `measured`, note "value derived from 18 dBm input minus coupling loss".
- Paper: p.3 "continuous-wave on-chip optical power of ~11.5 dBm"; p.7 "stable for an hour at input powers up to 18 dBm, with output powers >5 dBm. This corresponds to a stable on-chip optical-power handling capability up to 11.5 dBm". The paper does not show the derivation, and 18 dBm minus the stated ~4.5 dB per coupler is 13.5 dBm, not 11.5 (output >5 dBm plus 4.5 dB gives >9.5 dBm). The 11.5 dBm is the authors' own deduction and the top of the tested range, not a measured damage or handling limit.
- Correction: basis `author_estimate` (convention (h)); delete the "18 dBm minus coupling loss" claim from the note (or quote the paper's sentence); keep `approx`; consider noting that 11.5 dBm is a tested-to value (`gt`-like), not a limit.

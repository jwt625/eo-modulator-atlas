## Findings

### Blocking

**F1 (blocking). taghavi2022a-a `vpil_dc_vcm` = 0.12 (qualifier `lt`, basis author_estimate) is a bound from the abstract that the paper's own body data contradict by a factor of 4.**
- Cell: `data/_staging/p3_18/devices.csv` taghavi2022a-a `vpil_dc_vcm`, `vpi_basis`, `qualifiers` (`vpil_dc_vcm:lt`); evidence entry taghavi2022a-a vpil_dc_vcm (p.1 abstract).
- Paper: abstract p.1 "Measurement results indicate that VpiL's below 1.2 V.mm are possible for balanced Mach-Zehnder modulators" (a statement of what is possible, not a Vpi of the device in Fig. 11). The only measurement of the 1 mm device is Fig. 11, p.12: shift 120 pm/V at FSR about 1.15 nm. Vpi = FSR / (2 x 0.12 nm/V) = 4.79 V, i.e. VpiL = 4.8 V.mm = 0.48 V cm for the 1 mm arm; the ratio to the abstract bound is 3.99. My own reading of the Fig. 11 render puts the two nulls at about 1619.57 and 1620.87 nm (spacing about 1.3 nm, not the 1.15 nm label), which would give about 5.4 V.mm; either way the device is 4 to 4.5 times above the bound. Table 1 (p.5) assumes a 0.5 mm model arm and r33 100 pm/V; the body states no measured Vpi anywhere.
- Why blocking: the row is the device row of a measured MZM and `lt` makes every plot draw it as an upper bound of 0.12 V cm; the 4.8 V.mm arithmetic exists only in the evidence `derived` list and in papers.csv notes, not in the row `notes`, so a row-level consumer sees a bound that the same source's data violate. The batch report itself offers to drop it.
- Correction: clear `vpil_dc_vcm`, `vpi_basis` and the `vpil_dc_vcm:lt` qualifier (and the evidence entry) on taghavi2022a-a; keep the abstract claim and the 4.8 V.mm / 4.79 V arithmetic (formula, FSR and shift inputs, 1 mm length, factor 4.0, and the 1.15 vs about 1.3 nm FSR reading caveat) in the row `notes`; keep the `derived` list as is. taghavi2022a-sim (0.035 V cm approx, 39 GHz, `simulated`, p.14 text "predict a bandwidth of 39 GHz ... VpiL about 0.35 V.mm" and Conclusion) is correct.

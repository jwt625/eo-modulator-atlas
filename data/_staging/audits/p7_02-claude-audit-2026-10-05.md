## Rulings on the coordinator's points

1. **Overlap with canonical KIT SOH rows (schwarzenberger2026, kholeif2026, kotz2026, johnson2025; also staged singer2025).** No device is reported twice.
   - schwarzenberger2026a-a: O-band 500 um MZM, SOXD-224, Vpi 0.92 V at 1310 nm. schwarzenberger2026-a is C-band 280 um SOXD-100 (Vpi 2.4 V); kotz2026-a is a C-band 1 mm IQ modulator (SOXD123); kholeif2026-a a racetrack; johnson2025 rows are NLM Photonics/AMF DR8 PICs (Selerion-HTX, differential GSGSG); singer2025-a (p6_04) is a C-band 1 mm packaged MZM. The same device family appears in OFC 2024 paper Th4B.6 (ref. 22, identical title, p.13), which is not in the database: correctly flagged, not added.
   - schwarzenberger2023 (HLD, ECOC 2023) and schwarzenberger2023a (Perkinamine 5G, cryogenic, CLEO 2023) have no counterpart in canonical or staged data (searched all `eo_polymer` rows for o_band, cryogenic and crosslinked tags). The ECOC 2022 cryogenic paper (ref. 7 of 2023a) and the ECOC 2022 thermally stable paper (ref. 7 of 2023) are not in the database.
2. **Overlap with NTT InP rows (ogiso2016, ogiso2024).** No duplicate. ogiso2020-a (4.0 mm CL-TWE, Vpi 1.5 V, about 80 GHz) differs from ogiso2024-a/b (3.6 mm, >100 GHz), ogiso2024-d (3.6 mm, 2.0 V, about 70 GHz, previous-generation commercial PIC) and ogiso2016-a (3 mm, 2.0 V, single-ended). ogiso2024 cites this paper as ref. [3] (ogiso2024 text, references). The 2017 JLT paper (ref. [11], 67 GHz / 1.5 V) and OFC 2019 M2F.2 (ref. [8]) are not in the database.
3. **schwarzenberger2023 rows without absolute Vpi and the aged-device row.** Correct as staged.
   - Row a: the paper gives only "UpiL of 1.9 Vmm ... for the 1mm-long device at a wavelength of 1550 nm" (p.2). Entering vpil_dc_vcm 0.19 (unit conversion in `derived`, same pattern as canonical schwarzenberger2026-a 0.67 V mm -> 0.067) and leaving vpi_dc_v empty is right; Upi is never printed.
   - Row b: a separate row is right under (d)/(z). Its link data were taken "using the devices at the end of the high-temperature storage test (2256 h @ 120 C)" (p.3), a different material state from row a, and the paper does not say the 1.9 V mm device is the stored or measured one ("one batch ... 85 C", "The second batch of devices was stored at 120 C", p.2). Putting the link data on row a would mix states. Leaving physical_device_id empty and keeping the normalized Upi increase (about 1.27 after 1000 h, 1.30 after 2000 h, p.2, Fig. 1(c)) in notes is correct; an absolute aged Vpi would need the unstated device identity. See m4 for optional geometry repetition.
4. **schwarzenberger2023a physical_device_id across the 11 K and RT rows; temperature_k.** Correct.
   - "a pi-voltage of Upi=0.9V was measured both at RT and at 11K with a 1mm long device" (p.1) supports one physical device with two temperature operating points ((d), (z)), so the shared `schwarzenberger2023a-1mm` is right. Using the device in the transmission experiment is implied ("The MZM is mounted into the cryostat"); the notes say its length is not separately stated. That is acceptable.
   - temperature_k 11 = "The base temperature of the cryostat amounts to 11 K" (p.1); the paper reports all cryogenic data "at 11 K" (Fig. 1 caption, "11K 30G" labels). Accept it with the existing note. The RT row stays without temperature_k because no number is printed.
   - Vpi 0.92 V (abstract) on the 11 K row and 0.9 V (body) on the RT row is acceptable: the notes on both rows say the paper states the same Upi at both temperatures and the 0.02 V is rounding.
5. **ogiso2020 drive / vpi_convention.** Change to series push-pull: finding M1. The paper's word is "differential" ("differential GSSG line", p.2; "Differential drive characteristics are shown in Fig. 6", p.3). The topology it draws, however, is the series push-pull of ogiso2024: Fig. 2(b) shows both ridges on one common n-layer, Fig. 7(a) shows the two arms as diodes in series between the two differential driver outputs with the modulator bias on the common node, and the Fig. 6 caption reads "ER characteristics (push-pull drive)". ogiso2024 (same group, same n-i-p-n CL-TWE twin-IQ) states "Differential input / series push-pull drive" and is canonical as series_push_pull / mzm_series_push_pull. The Vpi number is unchanged: it is the voltage between the two signal lines in both readings. Only the label changes, so the label of the same Vpi quantity agrees across ogiso2016/2020/2024.
6. **ogiso2020 IL 8.5 dB scope undefined.** Correct under (s): "the overall on chip loss of 8.5 dB (each polarization) was estimated" (p.3), with no definition beyond the about 1 dB band-edge excess. The basis `author_estimate` is right (h). Wording fixes are in m2.
7. **ogiso2020-b EO response including driver gain peaking; bw_method.** Keep `eo_s21`. Fig. 12(b) (p.4) is a small-signal electrical-to-optical response of the driver-modulator sub-assembly. `link_eoe` under (t) means an E-O-E link response through a receiver, which this is not. The driver contribution is a statement about the device under test, not about the method. It is already carried by the row label ("sub-assembly"), the tag `driver_integrated` and the notes. bw3db 67 with gt and bw_measured_to 67 match "A 3-dB EO bandwidth exceeding 67 GHz was obtained" (p.5); the Fig. 12(b) axis and trace end at about 67 GHz, with a peak of about +5 dB near 55-58 GHz. No change.
8. **ogiso2020 published_on 2019-06-24 / year 2019.** Correct under (n). The cached version of record prints "Date of publication June 24, 2019" (p.1 footnote), and `scripts/refresh_metadata.py` `printed_online()` matches exactly this pattern ("Date of publication"), so the coordinator's printed-date candidate reproduces 2019-06-24. Crossref has only published-print/issued 2020-01-15 (no published-online). year 2019 = earliest candidate. paper_id ogiso2020 stays: ids are stable keys, and 30 canonical papers already have an id year that differs from `year` (e.g. witmer2020 -> 2019, zwickel2020 -> 2019).
9. **ogiso2020 authors in Crossref order.** Correct under (n). The staged list equals the crossref.json author order and membership (15 names, full given names). The printed byline order (Ogiso, Ozaki, Ueda, Wakita, Nagatani, Yamazaki, Nakamura, Kobayashi, Kanazawa, Hashizume, Tanobe, Nunoya, Ida, Miyamoto, Ishikawa; p.1) differs, and the papers note says so.
